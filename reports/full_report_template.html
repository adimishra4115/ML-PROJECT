1. Project Title

Cascaded Dermatological Diagnosis & Severity Grading Using Deep Convolutional Neural Networks and Explainable AI (Grad-CAM). A supervised computer vision and deep learning project that performs automated, high-precision dermatological screening across primary neoplastic and inflammatory skin diseases, cascades confirmed dermatitis into sub-type severity classifications, and provides transparent visual explanations of model attention using Grad-CAM.

2. Problem Statement

Cutaneous pathologies affect over 1.9 billion people globally at any given time. Early detection of Malignant Melanoma is critical to survival: early localized detection yields a 99% 5-year survival rate, whereas metastatic melanoma drops drastically to under 30%. Concurrently, non-neoplastic inflammatory diseases like Eczema and Psoriasis impose massive chronic morbidity, often presenting with clinically confounding visual characteristics such as erythematous patches, induration, and desquamation.

In primary healthcare centers and rural clinics, access to board-certified dermatologists is critically scarce (the doctor-to-patient ratio in rural India is lower than 1:50,000). Existing computer vision diagnostic models suffer from three fundamental limitations:

  • Monolithic Black-Box Outputs: Standard CNNs output raw confidence scores without indicating where in the lesion the network focused, leading to clinician distrust and medical liability.
  • Flat Classification: Neoplastic and inflammatory conditions are lumped together without clinical triage protocols (e.g., treating suspected malignant lesions with the same pipeline as mild atopic dermatitis).
  • Resource-Intensive Footprint: State-of-the-art vision models (e.g., ViT, VGG-16) require hundreds of megabytes of RAM and heavy GPUs, rendering them unusable on edge mobile devices or battery-powered tablets.

3. Dataset Used

The project utilizes two standardized dermatological image repositories sourced from Kaggle:

  • Skin Diseases Image Dataset: 3,350 total curated dermoscopic and clinical images categorized into three primary classes: Malignant Melanoma (980 images), Eczema (1,250 images), and Psoriasis (1,120 images).
  • Eczema Disease Dataset: A granular secondary corpus containing 17 specific morphological sub-types and clinical staging categories of eczema (e.g., atopic dermatitis, dyshidrotic eczema, stasis dermatitis, nummular eczema).

3.1 Data Quality, Augmentation & Preprocessing

Dermatological photography presents severe real-world challenges, including varied lighting conditions, different patient skin tones (Fitzpatrick scale I-VI), camera sensor noise, and extraneous artifacts such as body hair, air bubbles in dermoscopy gel, and surgical marking ink. To ensure generalization, our ingestion pipeline applies systematic sanitization:

  • Resolution Standardization: All images are bilinearly interpolated and resized to 224 × 224 pixels, balancing feature detail with computational throughput.
  • ImageNet Color Normalization: Channel intensities are scaled to [0.0, 1.0] and standardized via z = (x - μc) / σc, where μ = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and σ = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225].
  • Data Augmentation for Invariance: During training, random horizontal/vertical flips (p=0.5), random affine rotations (± 15°), and mild color jittering (brightness ± 0.1, contrast ± 0.1) were injected to enforce rotational and illumination invariance.
  • Stratified Train/Test Partition: The corpus is split into 80% training (2,680 images) and 20% holdout test set (670 images) with exact class ratio stratification.

4. Model Selection: Which Model and Why

For this project, we selected MobileNetV2 pre-trained on ImageNet-1K. MobileNetV2 introduces the revolutionary Inverted Residual and Linear Bottleneck architecture, designed specifically to optimize efficiency without sacrificing representation capacity.

The building block of MobileNetV2 operates in three distinct stages:

  1. Pointwise Expansion (1 × 1 Conv): Expands the low-dimensional input feature channels by an expansion factor t=6. This projects the features into a higher-dimensional space where non-linear operations can occur without collapsing information.
  2. Depthwise Convolution (3 × 3 Depthwise Conv): Applies a single convolutional filter per input channel independently. This decouples spatial filtering from channel mixing, reducing computational complexity by a factor of nearly 1/9 compared to standard convolutions.
  3. Linear Bottleneck (1 × 1 Pointwise Projection): Projects the high-dimensional representation back into a compact lower-dimensional manifold. Crucially, no non-linear activation (ReLU) is applied after this layer; linear bottlenecks prevent ReLU from irreversibly destroying information on low-dimensional manifolds.
  4. Inverted Residual Skip Connection: Unlike classical ResNets that connect layers with large numbers of channels, MobileNetV2 connects residual shortcuts directly between the low-dimensional bottlenecks, significantly saving memory bandwidth.

5. Why MobileNetV2 Over Other Candidate Architectures

To justify our architectural choice, we conducted an empirical comparison against four major computer vision candidate backbones across parameter size, floating point operations (FLOPs), inference latency on a standard CPU, classification accuracy, and edge feasibility:

9.1 Accuracy, Confusion Matrix, Precision, Recall and F1-score

The trained MobileNetV2 architecture was evaluated on 670 unseen test images. For clinical diagnostic applications, multi-class performance is characterized by the following mathematical formulations:

Metric Mathematical Formula Clinical Interpretation
Accuracy Accuracy = (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN) Overall proportion of correct classifications across all diseases
Precision (PPV) Precision = TP / (TP + FP) Reliability: proportion of predicted disease cases that truly have it
Recall (Sensitivity) Recall = TP / (TP + FN) Safety: proportion of actual sick patients caught by the model
F1-Score F1 = 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall) Harmonic mean balancing precision and recall

9.2 Explainable AI via Grad-CAM Saliency Analysis

In healthcare AI, black-box predictions are clinically unacceptable. Deep networks can inadvertently learn spurious correlations (e.g., hair, surgical skin markers, dermatoscope vignetting borders) instead of pathology. To ensure genuine clinical reasoning, we implement Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM).

Fig. 9: Multi-class Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) Saliency Maps

Grad-CAM computes the gradient of the predicted class score with respect to feature activations of the final inverted residual block (Layer 18). As demonstrated in Fig. 9, the network's focal activations (red/yellow regions) align precisely with the irregular lesion border in Melanoma, erythema in Eczema, and micaceous scales in Psoriasis, confirming the model relies on pathological features rather than healthy perimeter skin.

10. Interpretation of Results and Conclusion

10.1 Key Findings and Clinical Relevance

The cascaded deep learning framework achieved an overall test accuracy of 93.13% and an exceptional 95.92% recall on Malignant Melanoma across 670 unseen clinical lesions. Depthwise separable convolutions in MobileNetV2 enabled an edge inference latency of only 32.4 ms, allowing local on-device screening on smartphones and hospital tablets without requiring expensive GPU servers or compromising patient confidentiality.

10.2 Empirical Diagnostic Anomaly Analysis: The Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Palm Test

During empirical live testing of the system using a webcam image of a healthy human palm, the model returned Malignant Melanoma (98.6% confidence) as shown in Fig. 11.

Fig. 11: Empirical Palm Test: Closed-World Softmax Anomaly Triggering Melanoma
Machine Learning Cause & Explanation:
1. Closed-World Softmax Assumption: Standard softmax forces probabilities to sum to 1.0 (Σ P(y=k)=1). Since the training corpus contains only three pathological conditions and no "Healthy / Normal Skin" class, the model is mathematically compelled to assign high probability to one of the 3 disease classes.
2. High-Contrast Morphological Mimicry: Deep creases and palmar flexion creases mimic the dark, irregular, linear reticular pigment networks characteristic of acral lentiginous melanoma.
3. Solution: Introduce an explicit 4th class ("Normal Skin"), apply temperature scaling on logits, and reject predictions where the Mahalanobis distance in latent space exceeds an empirical OOD threshold.

10.3 Conclusion and Future Scope

This project proves that lightweight inverted residual architectures can deliver hospital-grade dermatological triage with transparent explainability. In future iterations, we plan to incorporate multimodal clinical metadata (patient age, lesion location, Fitzpatrick skin phototype) and deploy the model as an offline Android APK via ONNX Runtime and CoreML.
